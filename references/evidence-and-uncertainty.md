# Evidence and Uncertainty Reference

Use when the scenario contains incomplete information, conflicting information, or external claims.

## Evidence classes

### Known

Explicitly supported.

### Inferred

Reasonably derived but not confirmed.

### Assumed

Temporarily adopted to continue.

### Unknown

Cannot be determined.

## Rules

- Never present inference as fact.
- Never hide an assumption.
- Flag assumptions that materially affect safety, architecture, workflow, compliance, or acceptance.
- Do not invent local policy, vendor capability, clinical threshold, interface behavior, or approval.

## Claim-specific applicability

Separate binding authority, local adopted policy, observed practice, stakeholder reports, verified
product capability, and external recommendations. A report is known as a report, not proof of cause.
Check jurisdiction, setting, product/configuration/version, effective date, and the claim supported.
Applicable law constrains design; local policy describes adopted rules; observation/logs describe
actual practice; official product documentation supports capabilities; research supports recommendations.
Do not use unrelated higher-ranked guidance to overwrite evidence of actual local operations.

Record claim ID, source/section, date/version, evidence class, applicability, conflict, affected
decision, required verification, and reviewer role. Keep conflicting statements visible and hold
the affected decision pending resolution. Continue analysis of independent parts.
If current verification is unavailable, mark the claim unverified rather than inventing a citation.
