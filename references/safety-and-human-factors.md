# Safety and Human Factors Reference

Use when the workflow can affect clinical care, medication use, patient identification, or other safety-sensitive operations.

## Potential risk domains

- wrong patient;
- wrong medication/item;
- wrong context;
- missing or delayed information;
- stale data;
- duplicate activity;
- incorrect configuration;
- manual transcription;
- interface failure;
- device/system mismatch;
- workarounds;
- bypasses;
- alert burden;
- cognitive overload;
- downtime.

## Human factors

Consider:

- number of steps;
- repeated data entry;
- interruptions;
- task switching;
- display clarity;
- device placement;
- workflow distance;
- handoffs;
- training dependency;
- workaround pressure.

## Boundary

For consequential findings use hazard → condition/cause → workflow consequence → potential harm →
existing control → gap → proposed control → owner → verification. Distinguish causal hypotheses from
supported causes. Link the record to the workflow step and acceptance check. Keep formal scores
unset without the organization's applicable framework and evidence.

The skill may identify a potential hazard or missing control.

It must not independently declare a workflow safe or define unsupported patient-specific clinical thresholds.
