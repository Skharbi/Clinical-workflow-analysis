# Project and Business Analysis Reference

Use when a healthcare workflow request includes business need, value, stakeholder engagement,
governance, scope, delivery planning, change control, acceptance, or outcome evaluation.

This reference adapts broadly applicable PMI and IIBA practices to healthcare workflow analysis.
It does not replace clinical governance, local policy, regulatory review, safety engineering, or
formal project controls.

## Need before solution

Separate:

- problem or opportunity;
- affected stakeholders and workflow;
- desired outcome and value;
- constraints and assumptions;
- proposed solution;
- evidence needed to justify the solution.

A requested product, interface, device, or automation is a proposed solution until the underlying
need and alternatives are understood.

## Stakeholder engagement

Identify stakeholders based on their actual relationship to the change. Clarify who:

- supplies workflow evidence;
- owns the affected operation;
- makes clinical or operational decisions;
- defines or approves requirements;
- resolves conflicts;
- performs testing and acceptance;
- owns training, readiness, support, and outcomes.

Engagement should be proportionate to impact, decision authority, risk, and information need.

## Requirements life cycle

For significant requirements, preserve:

**Need → Stakeholder/Source → Requirement → Status → Design/Configuration → Risk/Control → Test → Evidence → Outcome**

When a requirement changes:

1. record the proposed change and rationale;
2. identify affected workflow steps and stakeholders;
3. assess safety, data, interface, testing, training, operational, schedule, cost, and value effects
   where relevant;
4. route the decision to the authorized owner;
5. update the baseline, traceability, tests, and affected artifacts after approval.

Do not call a requirement approved, baselined, or rejected without evidence of an authorized decision.

## Delivery and governance

For implementation planning, distinguish:

- deliverables from activities;
- dependencies from assumptions;
- accountable owners from participants;
- decision gates from status updates;
- risks from issues;
- acceptance from technical completion;
- deployment from operational readiness.

Do not invent budgets, dates, governance bodies, risk scores, or approval authority.

## Acceptance and value

Separate:

- **Verification** — the solution was built or configured as specified.
- **Validation** — the solution supports the intended workflow and need.
- **Acceptance** — an authorized stakeholder accepts defined deliverables or outcomes.
- **Benefits evaluation** — agreed measures show whether the change produced expected value.

Define measures with owner, data source, denominator, exclusions, baseline, target, observation
period, and review cadence only when supported or explicitly proposed for local agreement.

## Tailoring

Use the smallest amount of project and business-analysis structure needed for the request. PMI and
IIBA practices are adaptable foundations, not reasons to force every workflow question into a full
project plan or a fixed document set.
